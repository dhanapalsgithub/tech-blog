Tailwind gets a lot of criticism for producing "utility soup". The truth is that Tailwind by itself is not a design system — it is a set of primitives that lets you *build* one. The trick is to invest a couple of hours in **tokens** before you ship your first button.

## The Three Layers

A scalable Tailwind setup has three clean layers:

1. **Primitives** — raw HSL values in CSS variables (`--primary: 222 47% 11%`)
2. **Semantic tokens** — named intentions (`--background`, `--foreground`, `--muted`)
3. **Component classes** — built from semantic tokens via `@apply` or shadcn

## Why HSL?

HSL gives you two superpowers:

- Runtime theming without recompiling Tailwind
- Easy alpha overlays with `hsl(var(--foreground) / 0.6)`

## Naming That Survives Redesigns

Bad names describe **appearance** (`bg-gray-900`). Good names describe **intent** (`bg-foreground`). When your brand shifts from slate to zinc, your component code never changes.

## Takeaway

With tokens as the foundation, Tailwind stops being utility soup and starts being your design system's shortest possible expression.
